NAME
       lexconv - nylex documents to and from RTF, Word, OpenDocument, Markdown

SYNOPSIS
       lexconv [ -n | -a ] [ -w width ] [ -j ] from to

DESCRIPTION
       Lexconv  turns  a  document  of  nylex(1)  (and of LEX) into a file for
       another word processor, or such a file into a document.  The formats go
       by the extensions of the files:

       .rtf   Rich Text Format (Word,  LibreOffice,  WordPad,  TextEdit  ...);
              written and read

       .docx  Word (Office Open XML); written and read

       .odt   OpenDocument text (LibreOffice); written and read

       .md, .markdown
              Markdown; written and read

       .html, .htm
              a web page; written only

       .txt, .text
              plain text in UTF-8; written and read

       A  file  of any other extension is a LEX document (.brv, .ltr...).  Two
       of the other formats may be given as well: the text goes  from  one  to
       the  other.   In nylex, X in the document menu runs lexconv (the record
       *=CONV, the menu switch option /H): it asks for the document  from  and
       the one to, as BREV and BREV.DOCX, or RAPPORT.MD and RAPPORT.

   Out of a LEX document
       A  LEX  document  is  lines  laid  out  on  rulers;  out of it come the
       paragraphs again.  Lines that were broken by word wrap (the  next  word
       did  not fit before M) or justified (on a J ruler, out to M) are joined
       into a paragraph, which is justified when its  ruler  was  J.   A  line
       centred  or  put against M stays so.  A line of columns (pieces of text
       two or more spaces apart: a table, tab stops) gets  tab  stops  at  its
       columns,  numbers  ending  at  theirs (in Markdown, HTML and text it is
       kept as it is, in a fixed font).  .T and .F become the page header  and
       footer, with a page number field for (SIDA: or (PAGE:; .P a page break,
       .N  a footnote (at its place); the characters of .C for a pound sign, a
       space, a hard hyphen and a footnote's mark are turned into  those,  and
       the  other  print  time  lines  are  left  out.  Attributes 1 and 4 are
       underlining, 2 and 5 bold, 3 italic.  Markdown gets **bold**,  *italic*
       and <u>underlined</u>, a line of columns a block of code, and a centred
       bold line a heading.

   Into a LEX document
       The  paragraphs are laid out on an F ruler of the width given (the M at
       column 69, as in the document types of nylex's system files), a J ruler
       for justified ones, with an empty line between paragraphs  (unless  the
       file  has  empty paragraphs of its own); centred and right-aligned ones
       are put so.  Headings are bold, the first level centred.   Lists,  with
       their  marks, and quotations are indented; tables and text at tab stops
       become lines of columns; a page break is .P, the header and  footer  .T
       and  .F.   Underlining,  bold  and italic become attributes 1, 2 and 3.
       Pictures, footnotes, comments and fields other than the page number are
       left out; links keep their text and, after it, their address.

OPTIONS
       -n     the LEX document is in 7-bit Swedish ([ \ ] { | } the letters A-
              umlaut, O-umlaut, A-ring  and  their  lower  case),  as  nylex's
              Swedish system file keeps it.

       -a     the LEX document is ASCII and Latin-1 (the English system file).
              Without  either,  $LEXNAT  decides (1 or 0; nylex sets it), else
              -n.

       -w width
              the column of M for documents made (69).

       -j     paragraphs justified (a J ruler) whatever the file says.

ENVIRONMENT
       LEXNAT as above.

SEE ALSO
       nylex(1), lexspell(1).

NOTES
       Word and OpenDocument files are zip files: they are written stored (not
       compressed), which every program reads, and read  with  an  inflate  of
       lexconv's own; nothing else is needed.  The older Word format (.doc) is
       not read or written: Word and LibreOffice save .docx and .rtf.
